These are real photos from NASA's SPHEREx space telescope, one for each day it looked at this spot.

SPHEREx sees infrared light, which our eyes cannot; the three colours stand for three infrared bands. Dark brown patches have no data yet.

Very bright here: this object overloads the SPHEREx detectors, so its centre can look patchy or oddly coloured. That is the camera, not the sky.

Measured in this field

The object is found on the detector pixels of every SPHEREx exposure and compared with where the Gaia catalogue says it should be on that date. A straight-line motion is then fitted through the positions.

Brightness is compared between sky passes, about 6 months apart. A change of 3σ or more counts as variable.
